Sensory development is really important, and everything humans do involves one or more senses. By using their senses (seeing, touching, smelling, hearing), babies discover the world. Interpreting the meaning of these sensations is called **sensory processing**.

When a child uses his or her senses to discover a new object, they create a neural pathway in the brain. The more often they stimulate their senses, the more likely they are to create new neural pathways and strengthen old ones. **Sensory processing can even help to deal with trauma!**

## Try these activities

**Sensory rice.** Fill a Ziploc bag with 1 cup of rice and 1 teaspoon of vinegar. Add about ⅛ teaspoon food colouring and zip the bag shut. Squeeze and mix until the colour is spread evenly (add more for a deeper colour). Spread the rice in a square bowl and let your child play. Hide things in the rice, or let your child pick up a single kernel with tweezers or their fingertips.

**Slime.** *This recipe uses Borax, so make sure your child doesn't eat it.* Mix 1 cup Bostik or Ponal white glue, 1 cup of water and food colouring in a bowl. Dissolve 1 teaspoon Borax in ¼ cup warm water. Mix the glue and borax together and see what happens! Get your hands in there and mix some more. If it is very watery, pour some of the water off. It gets even better after a while in the fridge.

**Calm-down sensory jar.** Fill a clear bottle ¾ full with warm water. Add glitter, jelly beads or other small objects and a few drops of food colouring. Superglue the lid shut. Your child uses it like a snow globe, watching the objects move around. This is a great calm-down activity when your child needs time to cool down after a big emotion.

**More easy ideas:**

- Swing outside on the jungle gym
- Jump on a trampoline
- Fidget toys (spinners, poppers, fidget cubes)
- Threading beads onto a piece of string
