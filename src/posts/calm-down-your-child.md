It is normal for a toddler to have temper tantrums, but when your child is 5 years or older and still throws tantrums, you might consider teaching them how to control their emotions. When a child is overwhelmed, he or she needs to self-regulate these emotions and learn skills to help them calm down.

## My top tips for emotional regulation

- **Get down to your child's level.**
- **Acknowledge his or her feelings.** *"I can see that it makes you really sad when mommy says you cannot eat a chocolate."*
- **Sit with your child** until he or she calms down.
- **Give your child something to stimulate them cognitively**, like building blocks, sorting beads into different colours or shapes, or building a puzzle.
- **As soon as your child calms down, then you can reason with him or her.** Explain why you made a certain decision or why things went the way they did. *"Mommy said you cannot eat a chocolate now, because we are having lunch soon. As soon as you've finished your lunch, you can have a chocolate."*

We are all emotional human beings and even as adults we get upset sometimes. It is important to teach kids how to regulate themselves for when you are not close by to help them with this.

## Flipping your lid

Dan Siegel invented the hand model to explain how a child's brain works. When a child becomes dysregulated, Siegel says the child has just "flipped his or her lid".

We have three main parts in our brain:

- **The brain stem:** takes care of the survival parts of our body (like breathing and letting our heart beat).
- **The limbic region:** responsible for emotions and attachment to others. It works together with the brain stem to assess situations and decide if a situation is good or bad.
- **The cortex:** the outer layer of the brain, which controls rational thinking and reasoning.

### How does the hand model work?

![Dan Siegel's hand model of the brain](posts/images/hand-model.jpg)

Our brain stem starts "fight, flight or freeze" mode when we experience trauma or stress. When a child experiences a big emotion, the stress hormone (cortisol) is released, and sometimes it goes into overdrive.

The cortex then disconnects from the rest of the brain and leaves the limbic region exposed. When this happens, we say that we *flipped our lids*: we can no longer reason or think rationally, because our brains went into fight, flight or freeze mode.

**That's why you need to approach the child emotionally, not rationally,** to calm him or her down first before you start to reason with them.
